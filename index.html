CSIR NET Physics Preparation and Coaching Guide

CSIR NET Physical Sciences tests deep core physics, analytical problem solving and careful attempt selection under negative marking. This guide helps you see where you stand, plan your week around classes or work, and decide whether you need a full course, a test series, or a conversation with a mentor.

Example only — adapt it to your timetable

A sample weekly schedule for M.Sc. + CSIR NET

This is an illustrative plan for a student attending M.Sc. classes and labs, with about 2–3 focused hours on weekdays and longer blocks at the weekend. Working candidates can shift the weekday blocks to early mornings or evenings.

  • Monday — Core concept90 min: one sub-topic (e.g. perturbation theory). Derive the key results yourself. 30 min: standard problems.
  • Tuesday — Mathematical Methods60 min: a tool you need this week (complex analysis, special functions, Fourier methods). 60 min: problems applying it to physics.
  • Wednesday — Continue + PYQs60 min: finish Monday’s topic. 45 min: previous-year Part B and Part C questions on that topic, with full working.
  • Thursday — Second subject90 min: a different area (e.g. Statistical Physics or Electronics) so the whole syllabus keeps moving.
  • Friday — Part A + revision30 min: general aptitude practice. 60 min: rewrite the week’s results on one page and re-solve mistakes.
  • Saturday — Timed practice3 hrs: a full-length or sectional mock under exam timing. Practise deciding what to attempt and what to skip.
  • Sunday — Review and plan60–90 min: classify the week’s mistakes (concept, calculation, attempt choice, time). Plan next week. Keep part of the day free to rest.

Frequently asked questions

When should I begin preparing for CSIR NET Physics?

Many students begin during the first year of M.Sc., because the core syllabus overlaps closely with M.Sc. coursework. If you are starting later, give yourself enough time to cover the core once, practise, and revise before the session you are targeting. The exam is held in sessions announced by NTA; check current dates on the official CSIR NET website.

How should I use previous-year questions (PYQs)?

Use them in two stages. While learning a topic, solve its past Part B and Part C questions to see how concepts are tested. Later, attempt full past papers under timed conditions as mocks. Pravegaa provides CSIR NET Physics previous-year papers and step-by-step solutions free.

When should I start full-length mock tests?

Take topic tests as you finish each subject. Move to full 3-hour mocks once most of the core syllabus has been covered — then take them regularly, because attempt selection under negative marking is a skill that improves only with practice. The roadmap’s mock-review method shows how to learn from each one.

Should I choose a full course or only a test series?

Choose a full course if your main problem is understanding — you cannot solve standard Part B problems without looking at solutions. Choose a test series if you understand the material but lose marks to time, accuracy or attempt choices. If you are unsure, take one timed past paper first and look at where the marks went, or ask a Pravegaa mentor.

Not sure which path fits you?

Tell a Pravegaa mentor your current stage, how much of the syllabus you have covered, and how your last mock went. You will get a clear recommendation for your next step.
